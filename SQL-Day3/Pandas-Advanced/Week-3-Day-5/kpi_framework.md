# KPI Framework — Retail Business

## 1. Business Objective

The objective of this KPI framework is to monitor retail sales performance, customer behaviour, marketing effectiveness, and inventory efficiency. These KPIs help management make data-driven business decisions.

## 2. KPI Definitions

| No. | KPI                             | Formula                                                                         | Owner              | Measurement Frequency | Target                                  | Type    |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------- |
| 1   | Total Revenue                   | Sum of all sales revenue                                                        | Sales Manager      | Daily                 | Increase monthly revenue by 10%         | Lagging |
| 2   | Total Orders                    | Count of completed orders                                                       | Sales Manager      | Daily                 | Increase completed orders by 8% monthly | Lagging |
| 3   | Average Order Value (AOV)       | Total Revenue / Total Orders                                                    | Sales Manager      | Weekly                | Increase AOV by 5%                      | Lagging |
| 4   | Sales Growth Rate               | ((Current Period Sales - Previous Period Sales) / Previous Period Sales) \* 100 | Business Analyst   | Monthly               | Achieve 10% growth                      | Lagging |
| 5   | Website Conversion Rate         | (Completed Orders / Website Visitors) \* 100                                    | Marketing Manager  | Weekly                | Achieve 3% conversion                   | Lagging |
| 6   | Customer Retention Rate         | (Customers at End - New Customers) / Customers at Start \* 100                  | CRM Manager        | Monthly               | Maintain at least 80%                   | Lagging |
| 7   | Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) | Marketing Spend / New Customers Acquired                                        | Marketing Manager  | Monthly               | Reduce CAC by 5%                        | Lagging |
| 8   | Cart Abandonment Rate           | ((Carts Created - Completed Orders) / Carts Created) \* 100                     | E-commerce Manager | Weekly                | Keep below 60%                          | Leading |
| 9   | Inventory Turnover Ratio        | Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory Value                                    | Inventory Manager  | Monthly               | Improve turnover by 10%                 | Lagging |
| 10  | Repeat Purchase Rate            | (Repeat Customers / Total Customers) \* 100                                     | CRM Manager        | Monthly               | Achieve 30%                             | Leading |

**Note:** The targets above are illustrative business goals, not verified industry benchmarks. Actual targets should be adjusted using historical performance and business objectives.

## 3. Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

### Leading Indicators

Leading indicators provide early signals that may influence future business performance.

- Cart Abandonment Rate: A rising rate can signal checkout problems or pricing concerns.
- Repeat Purchase Rate: Changes can provide insight into future customer loyalty and purchasing behaviour.

### Lagging Indicators

Lagging indicators measure results that have already occurred.

- Total Revenue measures realised sales.
- Total Orders measures completed transactions.
- Average Order Value measures the average revenue per order.
- Sales Growth Rate measures historical sales growth.
- Website Conversion Rate measures completed orders relative to visitors during a period.
- Customer Retention Rate measures how well a business retains existing customers.
- Customer Acquisition Cost measures the cost of acquiring new customers.
- Inventory Turnover Ratio measures how efficiently inventory is sold.

## 4. KPI Monitoring and Action Plan

| Situation                           | Possible Cause                            | Recommended Action                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Revenue decreases                   | Lower demand or fewer orders              | Analyse sales by product and region                |
| Average Order Value decreases       | Customers purchase fewer or cheaper items | Review product bundles and upselling opportunities |
| Conversion Rate decreases           | Website or checkout issues                | Analyse the customer purchase journey              |
| Customer Acquisition Cost increases | Marketing campaigns become less efficient | Review campaign performance and spending           |
| Cart Abandonment Rate increases     | Unexpected costs or checkout difficulties | Simplify checkout and review delivery charges      |
| Inventory Turnover decreases        | Slow-moving or excess stock               | Review stock levels and demand forecasts           |

## 5. Dashboard Design

The KPI dashboard should provide a single-page overview of business performance.

Recommended visualisations:

1. KPI cards for Total Revenue, Total Orders, Average Order Value, and Sales Growth.
2. A line chart showing monthly revenue trends.
3. A bar chart comparing revenue by product category.
4. KPI indicators showing actual performance against targets.
5. A table displaying KPI names, actual values, targets, and status.

The dashboard should use consistent formatting, clear labels, appropriate chart types, and minimal unnecessary decoration.

## 6. Conclusion

This KPI framework connects retail business objectives with measurable performance indicators. Regular monitoring helps managers identify trends, investigate anomalies, improve operational efficiency, and make informed business decisions.
